# EarthGuard: An AI-Driven Soil Health and Carbon Sequestration Decision Support Platform for Precision Agriculture in Kenya
The agricultural sector remains a fundamental pillar of the Kenyan economy, contributing significantly to national Gross Domestic Product (GDP), employment generation, and food security. However, increasing pressures arising from climate change, soil degradation, declining soil fertility, erosion, and loss of Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) continue to threaten agricultural sustainability and productivity. In response to these challenges, this project, developed under the World Bank Data for Soil Health Innovative Challenge, proposed and implemented EarthGuard, an integrated Artificial Intelligence (AI)-driven precision agriculture platform designed to support soil health monitoring, carbon sequestration assessment, and sustainable land management in Kenya. The project was recognised as a runner-up in the international innovation challenge, reflecting its practical relevance and technological contribution to climate-smart agriculture.

The primary objective of the project was to develop a scalable AI and Machine Learning-based decision support framework capable of integrating soil laboratory data, geospatial information systems (GIS), remote sensing datasets, climatic variables, and predictive analytics to generate actionable insights for farmers and agricultural stakeholders. The platform was designed to address critical limitations in existing agricultural advisory systems, including fragmentation, lack of scalability, limited predictive capability, and inadequate access to real-time, site-specific soil health recommendations.

The methodological framework incorporated multiple heterogeneous datasets, including laboratory soil measurements obtained from KALRO and SoilHive, raster soil property datasets from ISDA Africa, rainfall data from NASA POWER, satellite-derived vegetation indices such as NDVI from Sentinel-2 imagery, Digital Elevation Model (DEM)-derived slope data, and Above Ground Biomass (AGB) datasets from GEDI L4B. These datasets were integrated within a geospatial machine learning pipeline to facilitate predictive modelling, soil health assessment, and environmental risk analysis.

A Soil Management Health Index (SMHI) ranging from 0–100 was developed to quantify soil health conditions across agricultural regions. Soil parameters including soil pH, total nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and total organic carbon were standardised and weighted using the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) informed by expert judgement and literature review. Composite soil health scores were subsequently computed using weighted overlay techniques implemented in R and Python-based geospatial workflows.

To support predictive analytics, Random Forest Machine Learning models were developed to estimate site-specific soil nutrient deficiencies and soil properties using spatial indexing and geospatial feature engineering techniques. The deployed prototype integrated latitude and longitude coordinates with spatial binning methods to generate predictive soil property estimates for users in real time. The system predicted critical soil indicators, including soil pH, nitrogen concentration, phosphorus availability, potassium content, and total organic carbon levels, which were subsequently used to compute the SMHI score dynamically. The deployed web-based dashboard further provided interpretable recommendations for fertiliser optimisation and soil management practices.

Additionally, remote sensing and geospatial machine learning approaches were employed to assess erosion susceptibility and land degradation risks through the integration of slope data, rainfall intensity, vegetation indices, and terrain analysis. Carbon sequestration estimation models were also incorporated using Above Ground Biomass (AGB) data to estimate carbon storage potential and support carbon credit monetisation aligned with Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) standards. The framework further explored predictive time-series approaches for monitoring long-term carbon sequestration trends and evaluating sustainable land management scenarios.

The EarthGuard platform demonstrated the practical applicability of AI, Machine Learning, GIS, and remote sensing technologies within resource-constrained agricultural environments. By integrating predictive analytics with user-friendly digital interfaces, the platform provided farmers with accessible, data-driven insights capable of supporting precision fertilisation, erosion mitigation, carbon monitoring, and sustainable agricultural decision-making. Furthermore, the inclusion of web-based dashboards and SMS-based advisory systems enhanced accessibility for both technologically advanced and low-connectivity farming communities.

The project contributes significantly to the advancement of digital agriculture, climate resilience, and sustainable land management in Kenya. The findings demonstrate that the integration of AI-driven predictive modelling, geospatial analytics, and soil health indexing frameworks can substantially improve agricultural productivity, optimise resource utilisation, support carbon sequestration initiatives, and inform national soil health and climate policy development. EarthGuard therefore represents a scalable and innovative framework for precision agriculture and environmental sustainability within emerging agricultural economies.
